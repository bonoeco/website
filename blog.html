Baseball basics

Match the glove to the job.

Infield

Smaller patterns and open webs support quick transfers and clean exchanges around the bases.

Outfield

Larger patterns and deeper pockets help secure fly balls and cover more ground.

Pitcher

Closed or discreet webs help conceal the ball while keeping a balanced pocket for fielding.

Catcher & first base

These mitts use special shapes for receiving, framing and repeated high-speed catches.

Training

Smaller training gloves encourage two-hand technique, soft hands and precise fundamentals.

Craftsmanship

How a glove is made.

  1. Pattern and cutting. Leather is graded, oriented and cut into panels.
  2. Skiving and preparation. Edges are thinned and prepared for clean joins and controlled flexibility.
  3. Stitching and assembly. Panels, linings and padding are sewn with attention to tension and alignment.
  4. Forming and lacing. The shell is shaped, the pocket is formed and laces are tensioned for the intended position.
  5. Inspection. Stitching, symmetry, finish, branding and function are checked before packing.

A short history

Why gloves evolved.

Early baseball players used relatively minimal hand protection. As throwing speed, ball design and defensive expectations changed, gloves grew larger, developed deeper pockets and adopted position-specific shapes. Modern gloves combine traditional handcraft with consistent patterns, materials and quality control.

Every design decision still comes back to the same purpose: helping a player receive, secure and release the ball with confidence.
